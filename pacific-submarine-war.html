Silent Service: The U.S. Submarine Campaign Against Japan

Operating thousands of miles from their home bases in Pearl Harbor and Australia, American Gato-class and Balao-class submarines waged a relentless campaign that ultimately crippled the island empire of Japan.

Strangling an Island Empire

Unlike surface fleets, U.S. submarines operated independently behind enemy lines, systematically targeting Japanese merchant shipping, oil tankers, and supply freighters. By 1945, the submarine campaign had starved Japan of vital petroleum, rubber, and food resources, effectively collapsing its war economy.
