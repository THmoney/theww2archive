Appeasement and Treaty Violations: How Europe Sat Back as Hitler Rearmed

Once established in power, Hitler wasted no time systematically shredding the military restrictions mandated by the Treaty of Versailles. In violation of international accords, Germany began secret rearmament, reinstituted mandatory military conscription, and dramatically rebuilt the Luftwaffe (air force) and Kriegsmarine (navy). When German troops marched back into the demilitarized Rhineland in March 1936, Allied forces held overwhelming superiority but chose to sit back and do nothing, emboldening Berlin's aggressive ambitions.

Bloodless Conquests: The Anschluss and Czechoslovakia

Having tested international resolve and found it weak, Hitler turned toward territorial expansion (*Lebensraum*). In March 1938, German troops marched into Austria, executing the Anschluss (annexation) amid cheering crowds, once again without a shot being fired. Encouraged by Western appeasement policies—famously epitomized by British Prime Minister Neville Chamberlain at the Munich Conference later that year—Germany seized the German-speaking Sudetenland regions of Czechoslovakia, before occupying the remainder of the Czech lands in March 1939.

The Final Crossing into Poland

Convinced that Britain and France lacked the political will to fight, Hitler signed a non-aggression pact with the Soviet Union in August 1939, partitioning Eastern Europe between them. On September 1, 1939, German armies crossed the border into Poland, prompting London and Paris to finally honor their treaty obligations and formally declare war, plunging the world into a catastrophic global conflict.
