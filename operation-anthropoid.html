Operation Anthropoid: The Daring Assassination of the "Butcher of Prague"

Among the most covert, high-stakes resistance operations of the Second World War, Operation Anthropoid stands out as a masterclass in covert planning and supreme self-sacrifice. Executed in May 1942, the top-secret mission targeted SS-Obergruppenführer Reinhard Heydrich—chief of the Reich Main Security Office, acting Reich Protector of Bohemia and Moravia, and one of the principal architects of the Holocaust.

The Shadow of Heydrich and the Czech Resistance

Appointed to govern the occupied Czech lands in late 1941, Heydrich unleashed a reign of terror aimed at pacifying local resistance and maximizing industrial production for the German war machine. Through widespread executions, arrests, and deportations, he earned the brutal epithets "The Butcher of Prague" and "The Hangman."

Recognizing that Heydrich's ruthlessness threatened to extinguish the domestic resistance movement entirely, President Edvard Beneš and the Czechoslovak government-in-exile in London resolved to strike back. In coordination with the British Special Operations Executive (SOE), two elite Czechoslovak operatives—Jozef Gabčík (a Slovak) and Jan Kubiš (a Czech)—were selected, trained in clandestine warfare, and parachuted into occupied territory during Operation Anthropoid.

The Hairpin Turn Ambush in Prague

For months, Gabčík and Kubiš lived underground in Prague, supported by a brave network of local Czech families and resistance fighters. After meticulously studying Heydrich’s daily routine, they identified the optimal strike location: a sharp hairpin turn in the Libeň district of Prague, where Heydrich’s open-top Mercedes-Benz would be forced to slow to a crawl.

On the morning of May 27, 1942, as Heydrich’s car rounded the curve without an armed escort, Gabčík stepped into the road and raised his Sten submachine gun. In a horrifying moment of tactical friction, the gun jammed. Reacting instantaneously, Kubiš hurled a specially modified anti-tank grenade at the rear of the vehicle. The explosion shattered the car's bodywork, driving metal fragments and seat upholstery fibers into Heydrich's side. Though Heydrich initially drew his pistol and gave chase, he collapsed shortly after and died of severe blood poisoning on June 4.

The SS Reprisals and Last Stand at St. Cyril

The assassination struck terror into Nazi leadership, prompting Hitler to order immediate, brutal vengeance. Acting on false intelligence, SS units completely wiped out the villages of Lidice and Ležáky, executing all adult men, sending women to concentration camps, and razing the buildings to the ground.

Following a betrayal by a fellow parachutist, the Gestapo surrounded Gabčík, Kubiš, and five other operatives hidden in the crypt of the Church of Saints Cyril and Methodius in Prague. For hours, seven resistance fighters held off over 700 Waffen-SS troops. Refusing surrender despite tear gas, flooding, and heavy gunfire, the last defenders used their final bullets on themselves. Operation Anthropoid demonstrated to the world that even the highest leaders of the Nazi regime were not untouchable.
