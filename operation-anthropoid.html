Operation Anthropoid: The Daring Assassination of the "Butcher of Prague"

Among the most daring resistance operations of the Second World War, Operation Anthropoid stands out as a masterclass in covert planning and supreme sacrifice. Executed in May 1942, the mission targeted SS-Obergruppenführer Reinhard Heydrich, one of the most ruthless architects of the Holocaust and the acting Reich Protector of Bohemia and Moravia.

The Exiled Czech Resistance and Training

Conceived by the Czechoslovak government-in-exile in London and trained by British Special Operations Executive (SOE) instructors, two Czechoslovak soldiers—Jozef Gabčík and Jan Kubiš—were parachuted back into occupied Czechoslovakia. Their objective was nothing less than eliminating a man widely regarded as Hitler's potential successor and the primary executor of the "Final Solution."

The Ambush and Reprises

On May 27, 1942, Gabčík and Kubiš ambushed Heydrich's open-top Mercedes along a sharp hairpin turn in Prague. Though Gabčík's Sten gun jammed at the critical moment, Kubiš hurled a modified anti-tank grenade, shattering the vehicle's rear and inflicting fatal injuries. The operation succeeded in eliminating a high-ranking Nazi tyrant, though it triggered brutal reprisals by the SS, most notably the tragic destruction of the village of Lidice.
